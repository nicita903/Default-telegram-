Four directions

Same five chats and the same conversation in every mockup, so you compare structure, not content. Each direction has a different idea about what a message is: a letter, an object, a light, a signal.

Direction 01

Paper

Correspondence, not chat. Messages stop being bubbles and become written lines on a page.

  • Warm off-white paper, ink-black serif type
  • No bubbles: hairlines, rules and a left accent stroke
  • Unread as a thin wax-seal ring
  • Mono micro-labels for structure
Watch out — without bubbles, grouping, replies and media need a fresh visual language, and the warm palette is hard to make feel "fast".
Direction 02

Depth

Objects on a desk. Every chat and message is a soft physical card you could pick up.

  • Floating white cards, layered soft shadows
  • Gradient avatars with a light-catching rim
  • Outgoing bubbles as tinted glass
  • Composer lifted above the thread
Watch out — closest to the mainstream. Heavy shadows cost real frames on long scrolls, and cards lower information density on small phones.
Direction 03

Aurora

Light behind the glass. A slow colour field lives under the interface instead of a flat black sheet.

  • Animated gradient field, black-to-colour depth
  • Hairline borders, no cards
  • Unread rows glow instead of shouting
  • Outgoing bubbles carry a colour rim
Watch out — nearest to your current Telegram Glass, and the most "AI app" direction; the animation must stay cheap, and text contrast has to be checked on every gradient stop.
Direction 04

Grid

Signal, not decoration. The interface admits it is data: indices, monospace, a visible baseline grid.

  • Technical mono labels and numbered rows
  • Cut-corner blocks instead of pills
  • Acid-lime accent on near-black
  • Prompt-style composer with a caret
Watch out — very distinctive but cold; monospace body text hurts long reading, and photos/video sit awkwardly inside a technical frame.

How to choose

Paper — if Vector is for people

Calm, warm, memorable. Nobody else ships a messenger that looks like correspondence.

Most distinctive · hardest to scale
Depth — if it must feel effortless

Instantly readable tactile UI. Safe for onboarding, media, and settings screens.

Most familiar · least distinctive
Aurora — if it must look premium

Best for photos, music and a "living" feel. Builds on what you already have.

Closest to today · risk of looking generic
Grid — if it must feel powerful

Strong personality, ideal for power users, terminals, downloads, sync status.

Boldest · weakest for casual chat

My own pick would be a deliberate hybrid: Paper's typography and quiet rhythm for the conversation list and message text, with Depth's card lift only where content is heavy (media, music, downloads) and Grid's mono labels for metadata. That combination is hard to mistake for any existing messenger — but it needs a decision on one primary direction first.
